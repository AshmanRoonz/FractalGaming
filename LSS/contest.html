Enter one track or both. They're judged separately, and entering both doesn't help or hurt either entry.

Track A · Written

Design Pitch

A short, reasoned proposal for one new feature, mode, ship, creature or system. We judge the thinking, not your gaming skill.

  • 400 to 1,000 words, plus optional sketches or diagrams
  • Individual entries or teams of up to 3
  • Works as a class assignment (see For educators)
Track B · Live

Tournament

A bracket of 3v3 matches, played online in a private room. Fleets are made up of teams of three; solo players get placed on a team.

  • Saturday, Nov 28, 2026, streamed live
  • Any laptop or desktop with a modern browser
  • Schools can enter a club team and play together in one lab

Track A

The brief

Play at least three matches first. Good pitches come from noticing something while you play.

Your assignment
Propose one change to Last Ship Sailing. Explain the problem it solves, who it's for, how it would work in play, and how we'd know it worked.
Length
400 to 1,000 words. Sketches, flowcharts or mockups are welcome and don't count toward the limit.
Format
PDF or Google Doc link. Put your category on page one; leave your name off (judging is blind).
Originality
Your own work. AI is fine for brainstorming, but the writing must be yours. Tell us briefly how you used it, if at all.

Prizes

What winning gets you

No cash, and no lottery. Winners get things that matter for a portfolio and a CV.

Your feature, built

We build the top overall pitch into Last Ship Sailing, with a design credit in the game and on the About page.

Studio session

Every category winner gets a one-hour online session with the developer, walking through how their idea gets built and what changed along the way.

Reference letter

Category winners and finalists can request a written reference that describes their design work. It's useful for college, university and job applications.

Design council seat

An optional, unpaid volunteer role: playtest new builds before release and join occasional feedback calls. Under-18s take part only with a guardian's consent.

Tournament champions get an in-game champion credit and are also invited to the design council.

For teachers, professors and school staff

Run it with your class

The Design Pitch is designed to double as a real assignment. It's free, needs no accounts, and you control every point of contact with students.

Where it fits

Persuasive and technical writing, computer science, media arts, and design and technology courses. It covers problem framing, user-centred design, evaluating tradeoffs, and writing for a technical audience. The rubric above works as a marking scheme as it is.

Student privacy

Students under 18 enter through you. You send in their entries, and we never collect students' emails or contact them directly. Winners are announced through the school. We only publish a winner's first name and school, with your permission.

Content

Arcade-style ship combat: ships explode, and creatures lurk at the edge of the map. There's no blood, no gore and no text or voice chat with strangers. Multiplayer uses private 4-letter room codes, so a class can play only with each other.

Tech needs

A laptop or desktop with Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. There's nothing to install and no sign-in. Single-player against bots works right away, which suits a lab period or a lunchtime club.

The fine print

Rules

Who can enter

Anyone, anywhere, of any age. Entrants under 18 need a parent or guardian's consent, and secondary students may enter through their school. Fractal Gaming's own family members may play in the tournament but can't win prizes.

How judging works

This is a contest of skill, not chance. Pitches are judged blind against the published rubric by the Last Ship Sailing developer and at least one outside reviewer with a design or teaching background. The tournament is decided by bracket results. The judges' decisions are final.

Your ideas and your rights
  • You keep the copyright in your written pitch.
  • By entering, you give Fractal Gaming a free, permanent licence to use, adapt and build any ideas in your pitch into Last Ship Sailing, and to quote short passages with credit.
  • Game ideas often overlap. Several people may propose something similar, and features already in development may resemble an entry. Only the winning entry earns the build credit.
Privacy

We use your contact details only to run this contest, and we delete them within 90 days after winners are announced, unless you ask to stay in touch or join the design council. We never share or sell them.

Changes and cancellation

If dates change, we'll update this page and tell registered schools and entrants directly. If the tournament can't run, pitch judging goes ahead as planned.
