warm CLOUD lab

Nothing here is painted. The sun heats the ground, the ground heats the air above it, and warm air is light: it rises, the surface pressure under it falls, and the air around flows in to replace it. Rising air cools until its vapour condenses into cloud; the cloud shades the ground and rains; rain evaporating into dry air chills it, and that cold, dense air sinks and spreads out along the ground as a gust front that lifts new towers. The streaks are the air itself.

scenario

air & heat

Air accelerates from high pressure toward low; temperature differences are what make the pressure differences. The background wind is the large-scale flow (0° = from the north). Rotation turns moving air to the right in the northern hemisphere; in a box this size it acts over hours, so watch a sea breeze swing round through the afternoon.

rain & sun

threshold = cloud water (g/kg) a cloud must hold before its droplets collide into rain: low = clean maritime air (few, big drops), high = polluted air (many small drops that won't rain). rain evap 0 = rain never cools the air, so no cold pools.

storm

Shear: the wind gains this much over the lowest ~6 km, blowing from the "wind from" direction (WK82's tanh profile), shown storm-relative. Lightning needs ice, so only the Thunderstorm sounding makes it on its own: its rate follows Price & Rind (1992) for the storm's cloud-top height, in real time, once graupel reaches the charging zone; x scales it. Thunder comes from every part of the channel at once and reaches you from each at the speed of sound - that spread is the rumble.

render

With the sun behind you: sunlit rain shows a rainbow 42° from your shadow's direction, and sunlit cloud tops show a glory around it.

LSS overworld view

The ship's eye at the game's scale and lens: the camera's FOV is VERTICAL (120° = 144° across a 16:9 screen). The ground fogs like the overworld's terrain; the clouds fade into their own haze, out to the distance set above. Around the start (the home region) is the real sim; beyond it the world is cut into ~4.6 km regions with soft wiggly borders, each showing one of the "far-field sims" (same scenario and settings, each with its own seed and ground) at its own offset, half of them mirrored along the wind, so the weather neither repeats on a grid nor in step. 1 = the one sim, scrambled (cheapest); each extra sim costs ~1.5-2 ms a frame (one step + repack) and only runs in this view. They take one step per frame, so above 1 substep/frame (and at night) the far field evolves slower than the near. Fly: WASD, E/Q up/down, Shift x10, drag to look, right-drag to slide, wheel for altitude. Pad: left stick fly, right stick look, RT/LT up/down, RB x10.
